# Art prompts

Grok image prompts for everything in the Keeps and Armory tabs that has no real show still. Each prompt is written from the books. Generate the image, save it at the listed target path, and it is ready to use.

- **Keeps:** the card switches from the "Artwork pending" placeholder to the new image automatically.
- **Weapons:** after dropping the file in, change that weapon's `image` in `src/data/weapons.ts` to the `weapons/got/` path.

Sigils needed no prompts; every house seen on screen uses HBO show art (see `public/sigils/got/CREDITS.md`).

---

## Keeps: Grok image prompts (book-only castles)

These castles never appear on screen in *Game of Thrones* or *House of the Dragon*, so each prompt is built from the books. Save each result as a **1248×832 JPEG** at the target path. The Keeps card picks it up automatically; until then it shows `public/keeps/pending.svg`.

Shared style, already included in every prompt: *cinematic, photoreal, 3:2 landscape, 1248×832, a still from a prestige HBO fantasy series, natural light, practical-looking sets and matte-painting depth, muted film grade, no text, no captions, no watermark, no logos, no modern objects.*

Composition tip: the card crops to roughly the middle 60% of the height on desktop, so keep the castle in the central band of the frame.

## Grok prompts: Armory weapons with no usable real image

The style is the same for every prompt: a photoreal studio or cinematic prop shot, 3:4 portrait at 864×1152, with the whole weapon in frame. Hold the weapon vertical with the hilt near the top third, because the card crops around 28% from the top and mobile shows only a horizontal band at that height. Use a dark, warm background (near-black brown, #1b1510) to match the site. No text, logos, watermarks, hands or people unless the prompt says so.

After you generate an image, save it to the target path and change that weapon's `image` in `src/data/weapons.ts` from `weapons/<id>.jpg` to `weapons/got/<id>.jpg`. Until then, the current images in `public/weapons/` stay in use.

### Dawn
Target: `public/weapons/got/dawn.jpg`

Book notes. Dawn is the ancestral greatsword of House Dayne of Starfall. It was forged from the heart of a fallen star, and it is not Valyrian steel. In Ned's fever dream of the Tower of Joy (*A Game of Thrones*), Ser Arthur Dayne "drew Dawn from over his shoulder and held it with both hands. The blade was pale as milkglass, alive with light." Only a Dayne judged worthy may bear it, as the Sword of the Morning. The House Dayne sigil is a white sword and a falling star on lilac.

Prompt:
> Photoreal museum prop photograph of Dawn, the legendary greatsword of House Dayne from A Song of Ice and Fire, standing vertical with the point down and the hilt in the upper third of the frame. It is a very long two-handed greatsword, nearly as tall as a man, with a straight, broad, double-edged blade that is pale as milkglass: an opaque, milky white, faintly translucent, with a soft inner glow as if lit from within. It is not chrome and not shiny steel, and it has no wavy edges, no flames, no runes and no purple tint. The simple, austere knightly hilt has a straight crossguard in pale silver, a long two-hand grip wrapped in white or pale grey leather, and a round silver pommel set with a small pale stone like a star. A very subtle falling-star motif is engraved at the center of the guard. Dark warm studio background (#1b1510), a low-key rim light, a gentle glow on the blade, a soft shadow beneath, and shallow depth of field. Cinematic prop photography, ultra-detailed, 3:4 portrait, 864x1152, full sword visible, no text, no people.

### Robert's warhammer

Robert was a huge man, and the hammer is sized for his two-handed swing. Valyrian Steel's licensed book-version replica (VS0004) shows a squared iron head with a stag engraved on it, a pyramid-studded striking face, a long back spike and a long dark haft. Use it only as a style reference, because the site text describes an oak haft.

Prompt:
> Photoreal museum prop photograph of King Robert Baratheon's great warhammer from A Song of Ice and Fire, standing vertical with the head at the top third and the haft running down. It is a massive two-handed war hammer. The heavy, blackened iron head is squared, with a pyramid-studded striking face on one side and a long, curved, tapering spike on the other. The flat sides of the head carry an engraved crowned stag of House Baratheon, worn and battle-dented. The long haft is dark oak bound with iron bands and has a leather-wrapped grip near the base, ending in a short iron butt spike. It is brutal and unornamented, with no gold, no jewels and no fantasy exaggeration. Dark warm studio background (#1b1510), low-key lighting with a warm rim light and a soft floor shadow. Cinematic prop photography, ultra-detailed, 3:4 portrait, 864x1152, whole hammer visible, no text, no people.
